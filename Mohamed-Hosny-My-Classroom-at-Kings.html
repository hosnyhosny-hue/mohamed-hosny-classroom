Arabic Word of the Week

Word of the Week
حُرِّيَّة
“Freedom” — the state of being free
التعليم طريق الحرية
ḥurriyyah · al-taʿlīm ṭarīq al-ḥurriyyah — “Education is the path to freedom”

Linguistic Note

Derived from the root ح-ر-ر, which carries meanings of freedom, clarity and nobility. Related words include مُحَرَّر (freed) and حَرّ (heat — the free, open sun).

Expression of the Week

على قد لحافك مدّ رجليك
“Stretch your legs as far as your blanket reaches” — live within your means; one of Arabic's most beloved proverbs.

The Beauty of Arabic

The word شمس (sun) is feminine in Arabic while قمر (moon) is masculine — the opposite of many European languages. Our Year 6 pupils debated why, and their theories were wonderful.

Our Projects

“Letters to Old Doha”

What would we write to the Doha of fifty years ago — and what would it write back?

The Process

Year 5 pupils interviewed family members about old Doha, studied archive photographs, then drafted bilingual letters in Arabic and English before designing aged-paper editions with calligraphic borders.

Student Voice

“I learned that my grandfather's Doha had more sand than glass. Writing to him made history feel like a conversation, not a lesson.” — Year 5 pupil

Teacher Reflection

The bilingual letters became the strongest writing assessment of the term — pupils chose which language carried their emotion, then translated the feeling, not just the words.

Year GroupYear 5 · Classes 5A–5C
Learning GoalsBilingual writing · historical empathy · calligraphy · interview skills
Final OutcomeA bound class anthology displayed at the term exhibition

My Teaching Story

Mohamed Hosny

I am the Head of Arabic & Islamic at King's College Doha, teaching across the primary and secondary phases. My classroom is built on a simple conviction: language is identity, and identity deserves to be celebrated daily.

“I believe the best classrooms are places where pupils feel safe to try, confident to question and excited to discover.”
SubjectsArabic · Islamic Studies · Qatar History
Year GroupsYears 3 – 9
InterestsCalligraphy · Arabic literature · bilingual literacy
In my classroomI want every pupil to feel their language is a gift, their questions are welcome, and their progress — however quiet — is worth celebrating.
